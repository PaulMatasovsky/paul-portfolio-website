# Week 1 — Classroom Teaching Record

**Session:** Week 1, October 2026 (exact class date not independently verified)  
**Status:** Delivered, based on my instructor account  
**Attendance:** Two students  
**Format:** Two-hour core lesson and optional third-hour AI lab

## What I Covered

We covered all the material I had planned for Week 1. I introduced the fundamentals through straightforward explanations, games, questions, and demonstrations rather than relying on a lecture.

- **Instructions and order:** I wrote one word per index card to make “take out the trash love mom.” Rearranging the cards showed how the order and specificity of instructions affect meaning.
- **Input, processing, output:** I used my imaginary role-playing game, **Adventure World**, for several explanations and an input/process/output quiz. A player's attack choice, for example, can be an input; the game applies rules and displays the result.
- **Programming representations:** I demonstrated the same robot-square sequence in Scratch and Python: move 15 steps, turn left, repeat four times. The point was to expose students to how the same logic can be expressed differently, not to expect independent Python coding in Week 1.
- **Participation:** I broke up the talking with games and activities. I tossed snacks to students who attempted answers, right or wrong, to encourage participation rather than just correctness.

## What I Changed in the Room

Several activities I'd prepared assumed four or more students. With two present, I improvised and adapted them. I didn't need the projector for such a small group. The index cards proved especially useful as a simple physical demonstration.

There were few student questions. I don't want to read too much into that: it was the first meeting and at least one student was new to this kind of classroom setting. I haven't recorded a formal assessment of student mastery.

## Optional Lab: AI Art

During the optional third hour, we experimented with **supervised AI image generation**. I used the prompt “make it better” to discuss ambiguity: better in what way? More detail, different colors, or something else? AI can interpret vague requests differently from what the person intended. This connected the AI activity to our earlier lesson on giving clear instructions.

## Reflection and Next Step

The main takeaway for me was to prepare activities that work with very small groups, not just larger ones. The class gave me several ways to revisit the same ideas: physical word cards, Adventure World, Scratch/Python, and AI art.

I'm planning to spend the first ten minutes of Week 2 reviewing Week 1 before introducing new material. This is a **plan**, not a record of Week 2 delivery.
