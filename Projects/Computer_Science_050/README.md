# Computer Science 050 — Introductory Teaching Project

**Project status:** **In Progress** · **Activity:** Active (teaching and curriculum development). Week 1 teaching record published.  
**Course plan:** 20 weeks, ages 10 and up (including adults), with two hours of instruction and an optional third-hour computer lab.  
**Teaching began:** October 2026. Week 1 is documented from my instructor account; later sessions are not yet documented.

## Why I'm Teaching This

I wanted to teach computer science in a way that a ten-year-old could understand, without making the material too basic for teenagers or adults who are also beginners.

I don't want students just copying code. I want them to understand what a computer does, how to work through a problem, and how to recognize when a program does something different from what they expected. We use examples involving games, robots, everyday situations, and AI to help connect the ideas.

## My Role

I organized the course, decided on the goals and learning sequence, and chose to start with Scratch before moving to Python. I also chose to revisit important concepts throughout the course rather than cover each idea once and assume everyone understands it.

I'm responsible for reviewing the lessons and teaching the class. AI helps me prepare materials, but it isn't the instructor. I'll document what was actually taught and what students learned when I have supporting records.

## Planned Learning Sequence

| Phase | Focus |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1–3 | Foundations, algorithms, input → processing → output, and instructor-led Scratch examples |
| Week 4 | Connect Scratch concepts to Python |
| Weeks 5–17 | Python and other computer-science ideas, revisiting earlier lessons |
| Weeks 18–20 | Final project, review, and presentation |

Students will meet ideas more than once, with time to practice and build on what they already know. We'll also connect each week's topic to AI, including why its answers should be checked rather than accepted automatically.

The optional third hour is for practice, experiments, questions, and extra help. Adults who want more programming practice can join that lab. All required instruction stays in the first two hours.

## Teaching in Practice

I taught Week 1 to two students and covered the planned material. I adapted larger-group activities for a small class, used index cards to illustrate instruction order, ran an Adventure World input/process/output quiz, compared a robot-square sequence in Scratch and Python, and supervised AI art experiments during the optional lab. See the [Week 1 teaching record](Week_1_Teaching_Record.md) for the details and reflections. This is an instructor account, not evidence of assessed student mastery.
