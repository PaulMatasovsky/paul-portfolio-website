# ADR-001: Switch from Excel to Google Sheets for Operational Use

**Status:** Accepted  
**Recorded:** October 26, 2026 (retrospective)

**AI Transparency:** AI assisted with reconstructing and drafting this record from earlier project discussions.

## Context

I originally started building the ETL workbook in Excel. Partway through development, I ran into a printing problem: blank rows appeared in the printed reports.

There was also an accessibility issue. Every company computer had internet access, but not every computer had Excel installed. Using the Excel workbook would require my manager to work from a particular computer.

## Decision

I restarted the implementation in Google Sheets and made it the primary version for operational use.

Google Sheets resolved the blank-row printing issue and allowed my manager to access the workbook from any company computer by signing in.

I later adapted the completed workflow back to Excel as a downloadable, secondary version.

## Alternatives Considered

- Continue with Excel and use a computer where it was installed.
- Keep working around Excel's printing limitations.
- Build a separate application, which would have increased the scope of the project.

## Reasoning

Google Sheets addressed both practical problems without adding a new application to install. Access from different workstations and dependable printed reports mattered more than keeping the original Excel implementation.

While working through the printing problem, I learned how to use an array-based formula in a single Google Sheets cell to populate an entire row or column of results. I used that approach to bring headers from RawData into HeaderMap. In this workbook, the same approach helped avoid the blank rows that had been appearing in printed reports. That discovery was a major reason the Google Sheets version worked better for the original business need.

## Consequences

**Benefits:** The operational workbook can be accessed from any internet-connected company computer with the appropriate account, and the Google Sheets version avoids the blank printed rows that prompted the change.

**Tradeoffs:** Google Sheets requires internet access and appropriate sign-in permissions. Maintaining an Excel version adds compatibility work and testing.

The Excel version still has the blank-row printing issue. It remains useful as a downloadable workbook, but it does not fully match the Google Sheets version's printing behavior.
