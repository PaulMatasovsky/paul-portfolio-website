# ADR-000: Use a Spreadsheet-Based ETL Workflow

**Status:** Accepted  
**Recorded:** October 26, 2026 (retrospective)

**AI Transparency:** AI assisted with reconstructing and drafting this record from earlier project discussions.

## Context

Manually sorting warehouse parts reports could take over an hour and was prone to errors. Different part-number formats made ordinary alphabetical sorting unreliable.

The solution needed to work with existing reports and be simple enough for managers to use without technical training.

## Decision

I developed a formula-driven ETL workflow inside a spreadsheet rather than building a separate application.

As the project grew, I separated the work into stages for column identification, extraction, cleaning, part-number parsing, sorting, report generation, and validation.

## Alternatives Considered

- Continue sorting reports manually or with a partially manual spreadsheet process.
- Build a separate application or database-driven system.

## Reasoning

A spreadsheet fit the existing business workflow and reduced the amount of training and setup required. Keeping the processing in separate stages made it easier to identify errors and correct individual sections.

The goal was for the user to paste the report, select the desired output, and print.

## Consequences

**Benefits:** The workbook is self-contained, requires little user interaction, and exposes intermediate results for troubleshooting.

**Tradeoffs:** The formulas became complex, the processing capacity is fixed, and behavior can vary between spreadsheet applications.

A database-backed application may be a future improvement, but it was not necessary to solve the original problem.
